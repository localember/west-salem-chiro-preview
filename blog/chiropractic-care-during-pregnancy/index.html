Chiropractic Care During Pregnancy

Why pregnancy is hard on the low back and pelvis, how gentle chiropractic care is adapted, and when to call your OB or midwife first.

November 11, 2026 · West Salem Chiropractic

West Salem Chiropractic BlogChiropractic Care During Pregnancy

Pregnancy brings a lot of changes, and many of them show up in the low back, hips and pelvis. Aches that build through the day, trouble getting comfortable at night, and stiffness when you stand up are common. They are also often waved off as just part of being pregnant. If you are expecting and wondering whether chiropractic care might help you feel more comfortable, this article explains what is changing in your body, how care is adapted during pregnancy, and why your maternity care team should always be part of the conversation.

First, talk with your OB or midwife

Chiropractic care during pregnancy is a complement to your maternity care, never a replacement for it. Before starting any new kind of care while you are pregnant, it is a good idea to mention it to your OB or midwife. They know your history and your pregnancy, and they can tell you whether there is anything to keep in mind.

Some symptoms are not mechanical and need your doctor or midwife right away, not a chiropractor. Call them first if you have bleeding, regular tightening or cramping, leaking fluid, a noticeable drop in your baby's movement, or severe pain that comes on suddenly. When in doubt, call your provider.

Why pregnancy is hard on your back

It is not only the extra weight. Several things change at the same time, and the low back and pelvis absorb the difference.

  • Your center of gravity moves forward. As your baby grows, weight shifts toward the front of your body, so the muscles and joints of your low back work harder to keep you upright.
  • Ligaments soften. The hormone relaxin loosens the ligaments around the pelvis to prepare for birth. That flexibility has a purpose, but it can also make those joints feel less stable.
  • Abdominal muscles stretch. The muscles that normally share the work of supporting your spine stretch to make room, and they cannot carry as much of the load.

Everyday life can add to it. Long stretches of standing or sitting, carrying an older child on one hip, and sleep positions that change week to week all put extra demands on your back and hips. If your low back was already giving you trouble before pregnancy, you may feel the changes sooner.

How chiropractic care is adapted during pregnancy

Chiropractic care focuses on the mechanical side of what has changed. When joints in the low back and pelvis stiffen under a new load, gentle and specific adjustments can help restore movement to them. The aim is to help you move, rest and get through your day more comfortably.

During pregnancy, everything is adapted:

  • Positioning. You should not have to lie flat on your belly, or on your back once that stops being comfortable or advisable. Positions are chosen to work with your bump, and they change as your pregnancy progresses.
  • Force. Adjustments are gentle and matched to your body and your trimester.
  • The plan. What makes sense in the first trimester may not be what makes sense in the third. The plan is reviewed as you go.

If a position does not feel right, say so and it can be changed. Nothing about care during pregnancy should involve gritting your teeth.

What a first visit looks like when you are pregnant

A first visit starts with a careful conversation. You will be asked how far along you are, what is aching, how you are sleeping, how your pregnancy has gone so far, and whether your OB or midwife has mentioned anything to watch for. This history comes first, before anything else happens.

At West Salem Chiropractic, Dr. Daniel Eoriatti uses the Gonstead Technique, which is built on careful analysis before any adjustment. The examination is adapted to pregnancy. If anything in your history or examination suggests you should be seen by your doctor or midwife first, you will be told that plainly.

For a general look at how first visits work, see your first visit.

When people tend to come in

There is no single right time. Some people come in early in pregnancy because they had back trouble before and want to stay ahead of it. More often, people come in during the second or third trimester, when the load really starts to change. Some come in after their baby arrives, when carrying, feeding and recovering bring their own demands. Care is matched to wherever you are.

Simple things that may help between visits

These are general comfort tips, not medical advice. Check with your OB or midwife if you are unsure about any of them.

  • Change positions often rather than holding one for a long time.
  • When sleeping on your side, try a pillow between your knees.
  • Break up long periods of standing into shorter ones where you can.
  • If you carry an older child, switch hips instead of always using the same side.
  • Wear supportive, comfortable shoes, especially later in the day.

Questions expecting parents often ask

Do I need my doctor's permission?

No referral is needed to book a chiropractic visit. Even so, it is a good idea to let your OB or midwife know, and we encourage that conversation. Your chiropractic care should fit alongside your maternity care, not apart from it.

Is it safe?

That depends on you and your pregnancy, which is why the first visit starts with a careful history and why your OB or midwife should be in the loop. Care during pregnancy is gentle and adapted, and if anything suggests chiropractic care is not the right fit right now, you will hear that honestly.

What if I have never seen a chiropractor?

That is completely fine. Many people come in for the first time during pregnancy. Everything is explained in plain language, and nothing happens until you are comfortable with it.
