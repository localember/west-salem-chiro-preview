What to Expect at Your First Chiropractic Visit

New to chiropractic, or new to us? Here is what happens at a first visit, how long it takes, and how to prepare.

November 11, 2026 · West Salem Chiropractic

West Salem Chiropractic BlogWhat to Expect at Your First Chiropractic Visit

Booking a first visit with any new health provider can bring up questions. How long will it take? Will I be adjusted right away? Do I need to bring anything? If you are thinking about coming to West Salem Chiropractic, this guide walks through what a first Gonstead visit looks like from start to finish, so there are no surprises when you arrive.

Plan for about 45 minutes to an hour

A first visit takes longer than the visits that follow. Most of that time goes into understanding what is going on, not into the adjustment itself. Dr. Daniel Eoriatti uses the Gonstead Technique, which is built on finding the specific area causing a problem before adjusting anything. That takes a proper look, so plan for around 45 minutes to an hour and give yourself a little breathing room on either side.

Step 1: Talking through your concerns

The visit starts with a conversation. You will be asked what brought you in, when it started, what makes it better or worse, and how it is affecting your work, sleep and daily routine. You will also be asked about past injuries, other health conditions and anything you have already tried.

It helps to think about what you actually want back. Maybe it is sleeping through the night, lifting your kids without worry, or getting through a shift without your back tightening up. Those goals help shape the plan.

This is also your chance to ask questions. Nothing is too basic. If you have never seen a chiropractor before, say so, and everything will be explained as it happens.

Step 2: The Gonstead analysis

The Gonstead System uses five components of analysis. Each one adds a different piece of information, and the goal is for them to agree before any adjustment is made.

Visualization

The doctor watches how you stand, sit and walk, looking for differences in the height of your shoulders and hips or the way you carry your head. This often starts before you even notice it, from the moment you walk in.

Instrumentation

A handheld instrument with two probes glides along either side of your spine and compares the skin temperature on the left and right. An uneven reading can point to an area of inflammation worth examining more closely. It does not hurt, and most people barely feel it.

Palpation

Next, the doctor examines your spine by hand. First while you are still, feeling for swelling, tenderness and changes in tissue texture. Then while gently moving each segment, feeling for joints that are not moving the way they should. This helps separate a stiff joint from an irritated one.

X-rays, where appropriate

Not everyone needs X-rays, and the decision is made based on your history and examination. When imaging is appropriate, Gonstead chiropractors use full-spine films that show the alignment and the disc spaces directly. That way, the adjustment is aimed at something that has been measured rather than guessed at. If you already have recent imaging or reports, bring them along.

Case management

Finally, your history and symptoms are put together with everything else. The five components are used as a whole, not one at a time, to narrow down where the problem is really coming from.

Step 3: Your first adjustment

In most cases, you will be adjusted on your first visit once the analysis is complete. If the findings suggest it would be better to wait for imaging or to check something else first, you will be told that instead of having the doctor go ahead anyway.

A Gonstead adjustment is done by hand and is aimed at the specific segment the analysis identified. Areas that do not need it are left alone. Your position depends on where the problem is and is chosen so the doctor can work on one segment at a time.

Most people describe a feeling of pressure and a quick movement, sometimes with an audible release. You will be told what is about to happen before it happens. If anything feels uncomfortable, speak up, and the approach can be changed. Some people feel a little sore the next day, which usually settles quickly.

Step 4: Your second visit and the plan

The second visit is where everything gets explained. It usually takes around thirty minutes and is more of a conversation than an examination. You will go over what was found, look at your imaging if you had any, and talk through a recommended plan with a clear visit frequency.

The plan is a recommendation, not a contract. You decide what you want to do with it, and you can ask questions or change your mind later. This is also the visit where payment options are discussed, so you know what to expect before you commit to anything. Our page on your second visit has more detail, and payment methods lists the options the office accepts.

How to prepare

  • Wear comfortable clothing you can move in easily.
  • Bring any recent X-rays, MRI reports or notes from other providers.
  • Make a short list of your symptoms, when they started and what you have already tried.
  • Write down any questions so you do not forget them in the moment.
  • Allow up to an hour so nothing feels rushed.

Questions people often ask

Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to book a first visit with a chiropractor. If you have questions about your insurance, ask the office before your visit.

Will it hurt?

Usually not. If an area is already very irritated it can be briefly tender, and some people feel mildly achy afterward. You are always told what is happening, and the approach can be adjusted if something does not feel right.

What if chiropractic is not right for me?

Then you should hear that at the first visit. Not every problem is mechanical, and some belong with a physician, a specialist or imaging first. Pointing you in the right direction is part of a good examination.

Ready to get started?

A first visit is about getting a clear picture of what is going on and what can be done about it.
