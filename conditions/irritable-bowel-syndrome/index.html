IBS

IBS care in West Salem

An IBS diagnosis usually means the standard tests came back clear and nobody could tell you why. That is the part we are interested in.

The short answer

IBS is a diagnosis of exclusion: it is what you are told you have once the tests have ruled other things out. That means you have a name for the symptoms and still no explanation for them. Our interest is in what might be driving yours, and in being straight with you about which parts of that belong with your physician.

If you have been reading this site you have probably noticed a theme. “The question you need to ask is not what you have but why you have it.”

What an IBS diagnosis actually tells you

It tells you the standard tests have been run and come back clear. You have been poked and prodded, you have a label, and you still have symptoms nobody has explained. That is a frustrating place to be, and it is where most people arrive here from.

The symptoms people describe to us usually come in some mix, in no fixed pattern, and shift over weeks rather than staying put.

  • Abdominal pain that comes and goes without an obvious trigger.
  • Abnormal stools, constipation, or diarrhoea unpredictable enough to plan your day around.
  • Bloating and frequent bouts of gas.
  • Stretches where things settle, followed by stretches where they do not.

None of that is in your head, and none of it is explained by the label on its own.

Why we look outside the digestive tract

A functional approach looks at your symptoms in relation to your whole body rather than to your gut alone, because digestive symptoms can be the cause of dysfunction elsewhere or the effect of it.

The things we most often find worth examining alongside IBS are the ones that sit next door to it rather than inside it.

  • Thyroid function, which turns up alongside digestive symptoms often enough to be worth checking.
  • Blood sugar regulation, high or low.
  • The gut microbiome, and the integrity of the gut lining.
  • Stress hormone patterns, and food sensitivities.

The connection between the brain and the digestive system matters here too, and it is the part a purely digestive work-up most often leaves out.

Where this fits, and where it does not

This is not a replacement for gastroenterology and we will not pretend otherwise. If your symptoms are new, worsening, or come with bleeding, unexplained weight loss, fever or night-time waking, that belongs with a physician first, and we will say so rather than book you in.

  • A long conversation about your history, not a seven-minute one.
  • An honest read on whether there is anything here we can usefully look at.
  • A referral onward when your situation calls for one.

We cannot promise a specific outcome, and we will not describe one to get you through the door.

About 12% of people in the United States have IBS, which is why so many people are handed the label and left without an explanation for it. You are far from alone in this.

Source: U.S. National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK), Definition & Facts for Irritable Bowel Syndrome. niddk.nih.gov
